about me

I grew up in Vancouver WA, across the river from Portland. In 2016, I moved to Albuquerque NM to get my bachelors in Physics at UNM, and graduated in 2020. I recently defended my PhD thesis at Rutgers University, in New Brunswick NJ, on building ML models for measuring dark matter in our galaxy.

Generally speaking, I am interested in any and all probes of dark matter physics. We know dark matter exists, but I want to know what it is! I mainly focus on astronomical and cosmological probes of dark matter physics, where there is tons of open data and analyses to be done.

In my free time I listen to music, work on my home lab, and play video games! Every day, I try to make my life a little bit more sustainable and independent from large corporate services. It's a long and often non-linear process, but it makes me feel good.

research

Much of my work on dark matter has focused on its signatures in the Milky Way. Dark matter halos formed in the early universe pulled in visible matter to make stars, galaxies, etc. Today, our Milky Way Galaxy sits inside a dark matter halo, giving us an incredibly laboratory to study it! We do this in two ways:

*Direct* detection: Look for scattering of dark matter particles off of visible matter. This depends on how much dark matter you expect there to be in a room at any given time. I measured this local density a few times, first in 2023 using a relatively crude solution to the six-dimensional Boltzmann equation, and again in 2025, where we correct for dust and provide a 3D map of the local potential.

*Indirect* detection: Looking for dark matter turning into some kind of visible "Standard Model" messenger, this could be light, cosmic rays, or neutrinos. I am interested in studying the Galactic Center GeV Excess, a long documented excess of gamma rays being emitted from the center of the Milky Way that might be produced by dark matter annihilation!

papers: (click for all NASA/ADS listings)

  1. May 2023 "ClearPotential"
    • Authors: Eric Putney, David Shih, Sung Hak Lim, Matthew R. Buckley
    • Links: arXiv:2512.09989
    • Summary: Using our powerful dust-correction technique developed in arXiv:2412.14236, ClearPotential upgrades the measurement of the local potential done in arXiv:2305.13358 to measure the unbiased three-dimension potential and mass density of the local Milky Way. This is a first-of-its-kind 3D map of dark matter, which is a powerful tool! We measure the local density of dark matter to be about 1/3 a proton/cubic centimeter (that's the amount of dark matter passing through you right now!), and are able to place constraints on additional components of dark structure like a dark disk. We try to constrain the global structure of the Milky Way's dark matter halo, and find some interesting results. Let's look out for Gaia DR4, with new data we'll be able to expand this map across much more of our Galaxy!
  2. December 2024 "Sweeping the Dust Away"
    • Authors: Eric Putney, David Shih, Sung Hak Lim, Matthew R. Buckley
    • Links: arXiv:2412.14236
    • Summary: Companion to ClearPotential! Optical surveys like Gaia are blocked by dust in the midplane of the Milky Way. When we measure the phase space of nearby stars, dust extinction *biases* this measurement and gives us incorrect solutions to the Boltzmann equation. However, the Boltzmann equation is just a continuity equation for phase space... What if you floated this missing fraction of stars as a learnable field, and find the solution that best obeys this equilibrium equation? Applying this to Gaia DR3, we are able to "Sweep" the dust out of the way and estimate the unbiased phase space density and local potential of the Milky Way.
  3. May 2023 "Mapping Dark Matter in the Milky Way using Normalizing Flows and Gaia DR3"
    • Authors: Sung Hak Lim, Eric Putney, Matthew R. Buckley, David Shih
    • Links: arXiv:2305.13358
    • Summary: We applied the methods developed in arXiv:2205.01129 to Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3), the most complete survey of stars in our galactic neighborhood. We measured a local dark matter density of about .47 GeV/cc, or about half of a proton of dark matter per cubic centimeter!
  4. May 2022 "Measuring Galactic Dark Matter through Unsupervised Machine Learning"
    • Authors: Matthew R. Buckley, Sung Hak Lim, Eric Putney, David Shih
    • Links: MNRAS, arXiv:2205.01129
    • Summary: In this paper, we demonstrate that normalizing flows are capable of modeling the complex 6-dimensional phase space of a simulated Milky Way-like galaxy (h277). We also show that our normalizing flow model is capable of accurately recovering the galactic acceleration and mass density field.
